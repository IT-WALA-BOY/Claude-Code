# Landing pages and marketing sites

A landing page is where a vibe-coded product loses most of its customers. Visitors judge quality within seconds, mostly without noticing they are doing it. Landing pages are about **presentation, not complexity** — the jump from generic to professional is a small, known set of moves.

## 1. Get out of template territory

These are the signatures of a generated page. Remove them first.

- **Alternating text-left / image-right sections repeating down the page.** This is the single loudest tell. Stack the hero centered, let it breathe, then vary section shapes deliberately.
- **Stock photography.** Delete all of it. Replace with real screenshots of the actual product.
- **Inconsistent CTA labels.** "Get started", "Try free", "Book a demo", "Get the demo" scattered across one page destroys the mental model. Pick one primary label and one secondary label; same label always means the same destination.
- **Emoji headings, glow shadows, clashing gradients.** Same rules as the app.

## 2. Page structure that works

A proven order — each block does one job, and there are far fewer blocks than instinct suggests:

1. **Hero.** Headline stating the outcome, subheadline naming what the product literally does, two CTAs (primary + demo). One restrained visual.
2. **Three core features.** Not eight. Three sections, each with a cropped product visual showing the feature actually working.
3. **Social proof.** Logos, reviews, or numbers.
4. **How it works, in three steps.** Onboarding narrated as a sequence. Three steps, not twenty.
5. **One summarizing "here is what it does" block.**
6. **Pricing.** As few plans as the business allows.
7. **FAQ.**
8. **Closing CTA / demo booking.**

If someone wants what you sell, this is enough for them to understand it and sign up. Everything else is delay.

## 3. Make it look expensive

- **Crop the screenshot.** A full dashboard makes the reader hunt for the point. Zoom into the one region that proves *this section's* claim.
- **Bento grid over a row of four identical cards.** Different content deserves different amounts of room; equal boxes flatten importance and read as a template.
- **Product-designer details:** a small badge on the hero, a row of customer logos, a mega menu in the nav. A mega menu quietly signals the product has depth worth exploring.
- **Real logos in full color** against otherwise muted chrome — this is one of the few places saturated color earns its spot, and it reads as enterprise-grade.

## 4. Copy: from what it does to how it helps

This rewrite is the single biggest quality jump available on the page.

| Descriptive (fine) | Outcome (better) |
|---|---|
| Collect and analyze your data | Turn your data into decisions |
| Schedule posts across channels | Never miss a publishing day again |
| Automated lead enrichment | Know who is worth calling before you call |

Same feature, promised as a result. Apply it to the headline first, then every section heading.

## 5. Motion

Marketing pages can carry more motion than the app, but it still has to earn its place.

- **Yes:** a subtle animated product demo showing the core loop working; a blur or crossfade as one panel transitions to the next; a menu that slides sideways instead of snapping shut.
- **No:** scroll-jacking, elements flying in from the edges, parallax, everything fading in on scroll.
- None of the good version requires custom illustration, 3D, or a freelancer. It is the components you already built for the app, arranged and timed carefully.

The test is the same as in-app: does this motion tell the visitor something?

## 6. Landing page checklist

- [ ] No alternating text/image ladder
- [ ] Zero stock photos; every visual is the real product
- [ ] Every primary CTA uses the identical label
- [ ] Screenshots cropped to the specific claim being made
- [ ] Headline promises an outcome, not a feature
- [ ] Three features, three steps — not eight and twenty
- [ ] One accent color; logos are the exception
- [ ] Pricing visible without a sales call, if the business allows it
- [ ] Reads cleanly at 375px wide
